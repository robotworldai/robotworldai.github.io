RobotWorld · Experiment report 01 · Preliminary

One agent, 84 robot tasks

Arms, dexterous hands, household robots, legged robots, cars and drones. Multimodal models, same tools, no demonstrations, no fine-tuning.

October 4, 2026 · RobotWorld team · Data snapshot 2026-10-04 12:52 (UTC+8)

TL;DR

  • 84 simulated robot tasks across 8 embodiment types and 5 task domains. Each task scores 0 or 1.
  • Two models have a result on every task: Astra 17.9% and Opus 14.3%. Opus's total includes 21 adjudicated results, all score 0.
  • K3 and DPSK score far lower: K3 2.4% and DPSK 1.2%. A few of their tasks are still running.
  • The two complete models solved mostly different tasks: they share 8 successes, Astra has 7 more, Opus 4 more. 65 tasks were solved by neither.
  • No model scored on household mobile manipulation (BEHAVIOR-1K, 0 of 10 for both complete models).

RobotWorld asks a simple question: if you give a general multimodal agent a robot as a set of tools, can it get the job done? The agent sees camera images and robot state, sends actions, and gets back execution receipts. A hidden evaluator, which the agent never sees, decides the score.

This is an early snapshot, not a leaderboard. Models ran in different batches with different reasoning-effort settings and non-action protocols. We report what happened, show recordings, and flag where the numbers are not comparable.

How we tested

SetupConfiguration
Tasks84 tasks per model, built on open-source simulators. By embodiment: 24 bimanual or dexterous, 20 mobile manipulators, 14 fixed-base arms, 11 wheeled vehicles, 4 bipeds or humanoids, 4 wheel-legged, 4 aerial, 3 quadrupeds. By domain: 48 manipulation, 10 household mobile manipulation, 11 locomotion, 11 driving, 4 aerial.
InterfaceEach robot is exposed as tools: observe (images, joint and task state), act (environment-specific actions, executed as control steps), and receipts. Auxiliary analysis tools are allowed.
ModelsAstra, Opus, Kimi K3 (K3) and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (DPSK).
SettingsReasoning effort: xhigh for Astra and Opus, xhigh or high for K3, high for DPSK. Current runs use 15 consecutive or 30 / 60 / 120 cumulative non-action limits per task; some earlier batches used other limits.
BudgetsNative step budgets are kept; BEHAVIOR uses min(official, 2000). Non-action limits stop an attempt that keeps looking without moving the robot.
EndingThe model has no give-up tool. If it ends a reply while the episode is still running, the control loop asks it to continue. An attempt ends only when the environment terminates or a step / non-action budget runs out.
EvaluationNative benchmark checkers, or world-state-v1 for 16 redesigned scenes. Some Opus and K3 results are scored 0 by protocol-rule adjudication rather than read from a native checker.
AttemptsOne attempt per model per task. Infrastructure interruptions are unscored, not 0.

A control step is one physical simulation step actually executed. It is different from the index of a logged event.

Results

Astra solved 15 tasks and Opus 12. K3 (2) and DPSK (1) solve far fewer.

Each square below is one task. Sorting by result shows how few tasks are solved and how few of K3's and DPSK's tasks are solved.

By domain, most successes are in manipulation, with a few in driving and drone tasks. Household mobile manipulation is still at zero for every model.

The two complete models solved different tasks

Astra and Opus both have a result on every task, so we can compare them task by task. They agree on 8 successes. Astra's 7 extra successes are mostly kitchen and tabletop tasks; Opus's 4 extra successes are dynamic tasks: catching a ball, a pushed quadruped, a drone with a hanging payload, and solo drone volleyball.

Success modes

Successful runs include long-horizon kitchen work and fast closed-loop control. Astra loaded a dishwasher in 949 of 1,800 allowed steps and drove a hairpin course. Opus caught a ball in a cup in 30 steps and kept a quadruped walking under pushes for its full 1,000-step episode.

Failure modes

Most attempts end at 0, but they end in different ways. Astra's failures mostly end when the environment terminates or the step budget runs out. For Opus, the most common way to fail is the non-action limit: 24 of its attempts stopped because it kept analysing without moving the robot. DPSK hits the same limit even more often, in 33 of its attempts.

Step use tells a similar story. Many attempts end long before the step budget is used: 46 Opus attempts and 26 Astra attempts used less than half. A step budget is a maximum, not a target, so finishing early is fine when the task is solved; most early stops here are not.

Below are two recurring patterns, each with recordings.

Looking without moving

The model keeps inspecting images and running analysis without ever moving the robot. On Bench2Dex dexterous-hand tasks, three Opus attempts hit the consecutive non-action limit with 0 control steps executed. The traces show only image views and shell analysis before the stop.

Household tasks stay out of reach

BEHAVIOR-1K tasks such as carrying in groceries or setting the table ask a mobile robot to navigate, open, pick and place in sequence. Astra used the full 2,000-step budget on all 10 and scored 0. Opus also scored 0 on all 10; 7 of those are adjudicated results.

Time

Median wall-clock time per attempt was 27 minutes for Astra and 51 minutes for Opus. This includes model thinking, API waits and simulation, and the two models ran different batches on different infrastructure, so it is not a speed comparison. We do not report API cost, because we do not have comparable cost records.

Limitations

  • One attempt per model per task. One more success changes a score by about 1.2 points.
  • Settings are not unified: reasoning effort, batch and non-action protocol differ across models. This is not a ranking.
  • A few K3 and DPSK tasks are still running; their totals may still change slightly.
  • 27 results (21 Opus, 6 K3) are adjudicated under a user-authorized rule, not read from a native checker. All are score 0.
  • Some early results predate the current ending rules: 9 Astra RoboDojo attempts ended when the model gave up while that tool was still offered, and some Opus and K3 RoboDojo attempts ended when the model stopped replying, before the continue prompt was added. They are shown as recorded. Opus and K3 RoboDojo tasks are being rerun under the current protocol; the 9 Astra attempts have not been rerun yet.
  • Simulation only. Real-robot transfer, safety and contact forces were not evaluated.
